Indian summer monsoon rainfall

The Indian summer monsoon (June–September) brings most of India's annual rainfall, so its year-to-year swings matter for water, farming and livelihoods. Explore all-India monsoon rainfall since 1871, and how it relates to El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD). Hover over a bar, or use the arrow keys, to read each season.

Changing links with ENSO and the IOD

The running correlation between monsoon rainfall and June–September Niño 3.4 or IOD values, over a moving window. Negative values for Niño 3.4 mean El Niño years tend to be drier. Values inside the grey band are not statistically significant at the 95% level.

About the data

  • Rainfall, 1871–2019: all-India area-weighted June–September rainfall from the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology's homogeneous series (306 rain gauges), as % departure from its long-term mean of 880.6 mm (IITM Monsoon Online).
  • Rainfall, 2020 onwards: India Meteorological Department end-of-season figures for the country as a whole, as % of the long period average (). The two sources use different station networks and normals, so values from 2020 onwards are not strictly continuous with earlier years.
  • Categories follow IMD: deficient below 90% of normal, below normal 90–95%, normal 96–104%, above normal 105–110%, excess above 110%.
  • El Niño and La Niña years: June–September mean Niño 3.4 SST anomaly (HadISST, via NOAA PSL), linearly detrended, at or above +0.5 °C (El Niño) or at or below −0.5 °C (La Niña).
  • Positive and negative IOD years: June–September mean Dipole Mode Index (HadISST, via NOAA PSL), linearly detrended, at or above +0.3 °C or at or below −0.3 °C. The IOD usually peaks in autumn, so some events are weaker in these summer means.
