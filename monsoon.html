Indian summer monsoon rainfall

The Indian summer monsoon (June–September) brings most of India's annual rainfall, so its year-to-year swings matter for water, farming and livelihoods. Explore all-India monsoon rainfall since 1871, and how it relates to El Niño, the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) and North Atlantic sea surface temperature. Changing links with ENSO, the IOD and the North Atlantic

The running correlation between monsoon rainfall and June–September Niño 3.4, IOD or North Atlantic SST values, over a moving window. Negative values for Niño 3.4 mean El Niño years tend to be drier; positive values for the North Atlantic mean cold North Atlantic years tend to be drier. Values inside the grey band are not statistically significant at the 95% level.

About the data

  • Rainfall, 1871–2019: all-India area-weighted June–September rainfall from the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology's homogeneous series (306 rain gauges), as % departure from its long-term mean of 880.6 mm (IITM Monsoon Online).
  • Rainfall, 2020 onwards: India Meteorological Department end-of-season figures for the country as a whole, as % of the long period average (). The two sources use different station networks and normals, so values from 2020 onwards are not strictly continuous with earlier years.
  • Categories follow IMD: deficient below 90% of normal, below normal 90–95%, normal 96–104%, above normal 105–110%, excess above 110%.
  • El Niño and La Niña years: June–September mean Niño 3.4 SST anomaly (HadISST, via NOAA PSL), linearly detrended, at or above +0.5 °C (El Niño) or at or below −0.5 °C (La Niña).
  • Positive and negative IOD years: June–September mean Dipole Mode Index (HadISST, via NOAA PSL), linearly detrended, at or above +0.3 °C or at or below −0.3 °C. The IOD usually peaks in autumn, so some events are weaker in these summer means.
  • Cold and warm North Atlantic years: June–September mean SST averaged over 35°N–55°N, 310°E–340°E, following Borah et al. (2020, Science), who linked cold North Atlantic conditions to monsoon droughts outside El Niño years. Computed from NOAA ERSSTv5 (area-weighted, via ERDDAP) and linearly detrended; cold if at or below −0.5 standard deviations, warm if at or above +0.5.
